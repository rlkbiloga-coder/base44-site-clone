Frequently asked questions

A template is a working app or website that you can copy and customize instead of building from scratch. Unlike a static design file, it ships with real functionality — pages, a database, logins and logic — so it runs the moment you clone it. Base44 templates are built by the Base44 team and by the community, and every one can be edited with plain-language prompts. Our guide to how to use app templates walks through a first build.

Base44 offers templates for both apps and websites, whether you’re getting your business online, making everyday tasks easier or starting a side project. Here are a few options to explore:

  • Business and productivity apps: Get organized with templates for task management, scheduling, customer relationships and inventory. Use our AI app builder to adjust the screens and workflows around how you actually work.
  • Games and interactive experiences: Have a playful idea? Start with a game template and use our AI game maker to make it your own. Customize the design, add challenges and give players something fun to explore.
  • Business and service websites: Find starting points for restaurants, fitness studios, real estate agencies and more. Personalize the content and design to create a website that feels like your business—not someone else’s.
  • Portfolios and landing pages: Put your work in the spotlight with a portfolio, or give a product, campaign or upcoming launch its own focused page where visitors can learn more and sign up.

You don’t need to find a perfect match. Choose something close to your idea, preview how it works, then describe the changes you’d like to make.

Using a template takes four steps:

  • Browse the gallery and open one to preview how it looks and works.
  • Clone it into your own workspace to get a full, editable copy.
  • Describe your changes in plain language, such as “add a booking form” or “change the colors to navy”.
  • Publish to a free Base44 domain, or connect a custom one.

Most builders have a customized version running in under an hour, because the structure and logic already exist.

The best starting point is the one closest to what you already need, so you change less. Consider:

  • Purpose — does it need logins and saved data (app), or pages and content (website)?
  • Structure — does the data model roughly match yours, or would you rebuild it?
  • Category — start in your industry; a scheduling tool beats a generic dashboard.
  • Completeness — check which screens and flows already exist before cloning.
  • Look — colors and fonts are the fastest things to change, so weight them last.
