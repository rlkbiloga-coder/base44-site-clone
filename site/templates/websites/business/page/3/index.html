Business website templates that get you contacted

Most small business sites lose enquiries the same three ways: no price, no easy way to get in touch, no way to book. These business website templates close all three by default. Services, proof, a price block, a contact form that lands in your inbox and a booking calendar are already built and connected. Updating a price takes a sentence rather than a developer. Publish, and the enquiry that arrives at 11pm is one you'd otherwise have lost. If you're starting from nothing, how to create a website with AI covers the whole process.

Frequently asked questions

A business website template is a pre-built site containing the pages a company needs in order to win customers: a home page explaining what you do, a services or products page, proof in the form of testimonials or past work, and a contact route. Better ones also include booking, pricing and a location or hours block. The value is that the structure is already decided by someone who knows what a customer looks for, so you fill in your details rather than guessing at a site map.

Fewer than most people build. The set that does the work:

  • Home — what you do, who for, and where, answered above the fold.
  • Services or products — what someone can actually buy, described plainly.
  • Pricing — a figure or a range. “Contact for a quote” loses enquiries.
  • Proof — reviews, past work, credentials, or client names.
  • Contact — phone, email, a form, and a map if people visit you.
  • About — short, human, and only if it earns trust.

A blog is optional and only worth it if you'll keep it up. Five good pages beat fifteen half-written ones, and every template here starts from that shape.

What no template can do is the rest of it: describing your services in the words your customers actually use, naming the areas you serve, and getting listed on Google Business Profile. They solve different problems, and plenty of businesses need both. A website is how customers find you and decide to get in touch — it's public, it's for strangers, and search traffic is the point. An app is how you run things once they have: bookings, jobs, stock, invoices, customer records, the screen your staff use. If you're not being found, start with a website. If you're being found and drowning in admin, an app for your business is the better use of a week — the App templates cover the operational side.

Choose on what you need a visitor to do:

  • The main action — call, book, buy or enquire? The template should make that obvious without scrolling.
  • Your industry's expectations — a trade business needs service areas and a phone number; a consultancy needs credibility and case studies.
  • Local or not — if customers come to you, hours, a map and directions need a proper place.
  • How much you have to say — pick a layout that suits your actual amount of text and photography, not an aspirational one.
  • What has to work — bookings, payments or forms: check they're built in before you commit.

Preview two or three as live sites and click through them as a customer would. Because these are working sites rather than pictures, you can fill in your real services and see the enquiry arrive before you decide.
