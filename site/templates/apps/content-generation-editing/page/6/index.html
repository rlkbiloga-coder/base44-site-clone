Frequently asked questions

A content generation app template is a pre-built application for producing written work, which you clone and adapt to your own process. It includes the parts a content tool needs: a structured brief, generation, an editor, versions, review and a route to publish. The distinction from a general writing assistant is ownership. You decide what a brief contains, who approves, what voice rules apply and where the finished thing goes — because it's your app. Changing it later works the same way: describe what's different and Base44 updates the app. That's the idea behind vibe coding.

Usually something narrower and more opinionated than a general tool:

  • A brief-to-draft app — your brief fields, feeding your prompt.
  • A repurposing tool — one long piece into the formats you actually publish.
  • A product-copy generator — consistent descriptions across a whole catalog.
  • A newsletter builder — assembled from links and notes collected all week.
  • An editing pass — your house style enforced, not a generic grammar check.
  • A localisation app — same piece across languages, with review per market.

Pick the one thing your team does most often and build that. A tool that does one job in your voice beats a tool that does ten in nobody's. See how that one tool fits a wider stack in our guide to marketing app development.

It can do both, and you should choose deliberately rather than assuming. Generation is genuinely useful when the brief is structured — a good app makes the brief the hard part and the draft the easy one. But the reliable time saving is in the management: one current version, a clear state, a review that actually happens. If you only build half, build that half.

Choose on the job you do most, not the widest one:

  • The repeated task — what does your team write again and again?
  • Volume — two pieces a month and twenty a week need different tools.
  • Who's involved — one writer, or brief-write-review-publish across people?
  • Approval — does legal or a client have to sign off?
  • Destination — where does the finished piece have to end up?

Clone the closest one and put two real pieces through it, review included. If your process doesn't resemble any of them, describe it to the marketing app builder.
