Productivity app templates that track who owns what

Tasks carry an owner, a due date and a stage, and you can set reminders to go out on their own, so following up stops being your job. Frequently asked questions

Productivity app templates are pre-built apps for running work — tasks, owners, due dates, stages and progress, already structured. You copy one and put your own projects in rather than designing the system first. The distinction worth knowing is whether it does anything on its own. A document with a task table records what you type; these enforce stages, notify owners and update every view when something changes. If you're weighing a custom build, start with this guide to productivity app development.

A to-do list is for one person and one dimension: done or not done. A productivity app handles the parts a list cannot — who owns an item, what it is blocked by, what stage it is in, and who needs telling when it moves. If you work alone on short tasks, a list is genuinely better and you should keep using one. The moment a second person depends on your progress, the list stops being enough, because nothing in it tells anyone else that something changed.

The pieces most teams end up needing, whatever the work:

  • Tasks with a single owner — shared ownership is the fastest way to lose an item.
  • Stages that match your process — not a generic to-do, doing, done.
  • Due dates and dependencies — so you can see what a slip actually delays.
  • Automatic reminders — nobody should be the human notification system.
  • A view per person — what is mine, today, without filtering.
  • History — what changed, when and by whom.

Start with fewer fields than you think. It's easier to add one when you miss it than to get a team to fill in six they don't value. The same logic applies across operations app development generally.

Choose on how your work actually moves, not on how the board looks:

  • Unit of work — are you tracking tasks, projects, tickets, or client jobs?
  • Stages — does the flow match yours, or would you rename every column?
  • Team size — a solo tracker and a twelve-person board need different defaults.
  • Deadlines — do dates drive the work, or is it a queue you pull from?
  • Reporting — does anyone need to see progress without asking you?
