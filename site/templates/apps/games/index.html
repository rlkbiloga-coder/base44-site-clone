Game app templates for challenges worth sharing

Put your own spin on an arcade classic, create a learning challenge, or explore a board game idea. Browse game app templates, from chess and snake-style games to interactive math adventures, and choose a starting point that sparks your imagination.

Use Base44 to create a game with AI and make the experience your own. Customize the rules, scoring, and design by describing what you’d like to change. Try a new theme, adjust the difficulty, and invite a few people to play before sharing it more widely.

Frequently asked questions

A game app template is a working browser game you copy and change into your own. It arrives with the machinery that makes a game shareable rather than a demo: a game loop, scoring, persistence so results survive a refresh, a leaderboard, and a link anyone can open. What you replace is the content and the rules. That split matters, because the infrastructure is the part that stops most hobby games ever being played by a second person.

Scores, streaks, unlocks and progress are stored per player, so closing the tab does not erase anything, and a leaderboard can be global, weekly or per group. Keep score submission validated on the server rather than trusted from the browser — the first thing a competitive player does is try to send a fake score. These are responsive web games, so a link opens and plays in a phone browser and can be added to the home screen — no store, no install, no review process. Design for touch from the start: anything needing a keyboard or precise pointing will feel broken on a phone even when it works. Games for children are better with no accounts at all — a nickname and a code rather than an email — because the rules on children's data are stricter than general privacy law almost everywhere. Avoid open chat, and keep anything user-submitted out of a game aimed at young players. App development for kids covers what changes when the player is a child.

Pick on how a session works, not on the theme:

  • Solo, one sitting — a puzzle or quiz; the simplest thing to finish.
  • Solo, returning — you need saved progress and a reason to come back.
  • Same room, many phones — a host screen plus join codes.
  • Turn-based against others — state on the server, no real-time pressure.
  • Live and simultaneous — the hardest; only start here if it is the point.
